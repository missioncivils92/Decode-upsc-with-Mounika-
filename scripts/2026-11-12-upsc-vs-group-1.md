## Hook
[On screen: "UPSC or Group 1?"]

Almost every student from Andhra Pradesh and Telangana asks me the same question in the first week: "Madam, should I prepare for UPSC or Group 1?"

For the last five years, I've mentored both UPSC and TGPSC aspirants. My answer is: you don't have to choose on day one. The two exams share most of their syllabus. What you need is one smart plan. Let me show you.

## Script

### 1. What Group 1 is (0:45)
[On screen: "UPSC → All India & Central services | Group 1 → State services"]

UPSC CSE recruits for the All India and Central services: IAS, IPS, IFS and others. You can be posted anywhere in India.

Group 1 is conducted by your state commission, APPSC in Andhra Pradesh, TGPSC in Telangana. It recruits for top state services: posts like Deputy Collector and Deputy Superintendent of Police, and other state Group A posts. You serve in your own state.

Both are serious, respected careers. Group 1 is not "UPSC lite". Don't let anyone tell you it is.

### 2. Side by side (2:00)
[On screen: comparison table]

| | UPSC CSE | TGPSC Group 1 (last cycle) | APPSC Group 1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prelims | 2 papers; GS I decides cut-off; CSAT qualifying | 1 objective paper, 150 Qs | 2 objective papers |
| Mains | 9 papers; 1,750 marks count | General English qualifying + 6 papers × 150 | Descriptive papers |
| Interview | Yes, 275 | Check notification | Check notification |
| Medium | English or a Constitution-listed Indian language (Mains) | English, Telugu or Urdu | English or Telugu |
| Calendar | Fixed every year | Irregular; depends on notifications | Irregular |

Two differences matter most.

One: the calendar. UPSC runs every year on a fixed schedule. You know Prelims 2027 is on 23 May. State PSCs don't work that way; notifications can come after long gaps, and sometimes schedules change. So you cannot plan your whole life around a state notification.

Two: the state layer. Group 1 has topics UPSC doesn't: your state's history and culture, the state economy and schemes, and in Telangana, a full Mains paper on the Telangana Movement and State Formation.

### 3. The overlap (4:30)
[On screen: Venn diagram — UPSC GS | Shared core | State layer]

Here's the good news. Put the two syllabi side by side and the shared core is large. In my estimate, roughly two-thirds of what you study for UPSC General Studies directly serves Group 1:
Polity and governance. Indian economy. Modern Indian history. Geography. Environment. Science and technology. Current affairs. Essay writing.

Take TGPSC Group 1 Mains from the last cycle: essay; history, culture and geography; Indian society, constitution and governance; economy and development; science, technology and data interpretation. Five of six papers sit on the shared core. Only the sixth, the Telangana Movement paper, is fully state-specific.

### 4. Where they differ in style (6:00)
[On screen: "Same topic, different exam"]

Same topics, but each exam has its own style.

UPSC Prelims questions are often analytical and tricky; you need concepts more than facts. State Prelims tend to be more direct and fact-based, with a lot of state-specific questions.

In Mains, UPSC wants wide, multidimensional answers on a national and global canvas. Group 1 Mains wants the same structure, but with your state's examples: state schemes, state data, state history.

So the core study is common. The practice must be exam-specific: solve that exam's previous papers.

### 5. My plan for both: core + layer (7:30)
[On screen: "80% core · 20% state layer"]

Here's how I'd do it.

Default mode, most of the year: roughly 80 percent of your time on the shared core, the way I showed in the 90-day plan. Polity, Economy, History and so on, plus the newspaper. 20 percent on the state layer: one evening a week for state history, culture, economy and schemes.

[On screen: "Notification mode"]
Notification mode: when your state's Group 1 notification is released, switch. For those months before the state exam, flip the ratio: state layer, state previous papers, state-style mocks. The core is already in place, so you're not starting from zero.

And when UPSC Prelims is near, from about March, go UPSC-first.

[On screen: "Telugu medium?"]
One more thing for those comfortable in Telugu: Group 1 lets you write in Telugu. If that's your stronger language, it's a real option for the state exam. But for UPSC preparation, keep reading in English; most UPSC resources are in English.

### 6. Which should you prioritise? (9:30)
[On screen: three profiles]

Honest answer: it depends on you.

Prioritise UPSC if you want an All India career, you're early in your twenties, and you can give it two or three full years.

Prioritise Group 1 if you want to serve in your own state, you have family responsibilities that make a long UPSC journey hard, or a notification is currently open.

Prepare both, core first, if you're not sure yet. That's most beginners. And it's a smart plan, not a confused one, because UPSC odds are tough for everyone. Selection is around 0.1 percent of applicants. Having Group 1 as a second serious path is wisdom, not a lack of confidence.

### 7. Recap (11:15)
[On screen: recap card]
- Group 1 = top state services. Serious, not "UPSC lite".
- Big shared core; the state layer is the difference.
- Same topics, different style: practise each exam's PYQs.
- Default 80/20 core/state; flip it when a state notification is out.
- Unsure? Prepare both, core first.

### 8. Next step (12:00)
Your task: download the last Group 1 question papers of your state from the commission's website, APPSC or TGPSC, and the last UPSC Prelims paper. Put them side by side. You'll see the overlap yourself.
