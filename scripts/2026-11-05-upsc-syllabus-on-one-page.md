## Hook
[On screen: the UPSC notification PDF, scrolling through the syllabus pages]

This is the UPSC syllabus. Pages of it. Most beginners open it, read three lines, and close it.

Today I'm putting the whole thing, Prelims and Mains, on one page. And I'll show you the secret that makes it far smaller than it looks: it's the same few subjects, repeated.

## Script

### 1. Why the syllabus is your most important document (0:40)
[On screen: "Syllabus = your filter"]

In my years of teaching, the students who improved fastest weren't the ones who read the most. They were the ones who knew what not to read. The syllabus is that filter. Every book, every news item, every video, including mine, you check against it.

### 2. The big idea: six core subjects (1:30)
[On screen: six tiles — Polity | History | Geography | Economy | Environment | Science & Tech — plus "Current Affairs" running across all of them]

Strip away the official language and UPSC General Studies comes down to six core subjects:

Polity and governance. History and culture. Geography. Economy. Environment and ecology. Science and technology.

And running through all six: current affairs.

Plus two special additions in Mains: Ethics, and the Essay. And your optional. That's it. Hold this picture in your mind; now let's map the official syllabus onto it.

### 3. Prelims syllabus (3:00)
[On screen: official Prelims GS I topics, each tagged to a tile]

Prelims Paper one, the official syllabus, roughly in UPSC's words:
- Current events of national and international importance → Current affairs.
- History of India and the Indian National Movement → History.
- Indian and World Geography: physical, social, economic → Geography.
- Indian Polity and Governance: Constitution, political system, Panchayati Raj, public policy, rights issues → Polity.
- Economic and Social Development: sustainable development, poverty, inclusion, demographics, social sector initiatives → Economy.
- General issues on environmental ecology, biodiversity and climate change, which do not require subject specialisation → Environment.
- General Science → Science and Tech.

Seven lines. That's the whole Paper one syllabus. Short to read, very wide in practice.

[On screen: CSAT topics]
CSAT, Paper two: comprehension, interpersonal and communication skills, logical reasoning and analytical ability, decision making and problem solving, general mental ability, basic numeracy and data interpretation at Class 10 level. Remember, you need 33 percent.

### 4. Mains syllabus (5:00)
[On screen: GS I–IV, each mapped to tiles]

Now Mains. Watch how the same subjects come back.

General Studies I: Indian heritage and culture, history and geography of the world and society.
→ History, Geography, plus Indian society.

General Studies II: governance, constitution, polity, social justice and international relations.
→ Polity, plus social justice and IR.

General Studies III: technology, economic development, biodiversity, environment, security and disaster management.
→ Economy, Environment, Science and Tech, plus internal security and disasters.

General Studies IV: ethics, integrity and aptitude.
→ New: Ethics. This one is about thinking, values and case studies.

Essay: any topic, two essays. It draws on everything.

Optional: one subject, two papers, its own syllabus.

[On screen: the six tiles again, now with Prelims ✓ and Mains ✓ on each]

See it? Polity is in Prelims and GS II. History in Prelims and GS I. Economy in Prelims and GS III. The same subject, studied once properly, serves both. The difference is depth: Prelims asks you to recognise facts; Mains asks you to explain, analyse and write.

### 5. Prelims vs Mains: same topic, two questions (7:30)
[On screen: split screen, "Prelims way" vs "Mains way"]

Take one topic: Fundamental Rights.

The Prelims way: "Which Article of the Constitution abolishes untouchability?" You need one fact.

The Mains way: "Discuss how the right to equality has evolved through judicial interpretation." You need understanding, examples, and a structured answer.

So from day one, study every topic in two layers: the facts, for Prelims, and the "why and how", for Mains. I've written Mains twice, and I promise you: students who prepare only for Prelims first find the switch to Mains very painful.

[Note: these are illustrative questions, not PYQs. Say so on screen.]

### 6. The State PSC angle (9:00)
[On screen: overlap diagram, UPSC GS and APPSC/TGPSC Group 1]

Now, many of you are also preparing for APPSC or TGPSC Group 1. Good news: the same six core subjects are the backbone of Group 1 too.

Take TGPSC Group 1 Mains in the last cycle: an essay paper; history, culture and geography; Indian society, constitution and governance; economy and development; science, technology and data interpretation; and a paper on the Telangana Movement and State Formation. Each 150 marks.

Five of those six map straight onto your UPSC tiles. What you add is the state layer: state history, the state movement, the state economy and schemes. APPSC works the same way with Andhra Pradesh topics.

So you don't need two preparations. You need one strong core and a state layer on top. I'll do a full video next week on preparing for both.

### 7. How to use this syllabus every day (10:30)
[On screen: three steps]

Three habits.

One: print the syllabus. Keep one page on your desk. That's the one-page map I'm giving you in the description.

Two: before you study any topic, find it on the syllabus. If you can't find it, ask why you're studying it.

Three: when you read the news, tag it. A Supreme Court judgement? GS II. RBI policy? GS III. That's how current affairs stops being scary: it's just the syllabus, happening this week.

### 8. Recap (11:45)
[On screen: recap card]
- Six core subjects + current affairs, everywhere.
- Mains adds Ethics, Essay, and your optional.
- Same subject, two layers: facts for Prelims, analysis for Mains.
- Group 1 uses the same core plus a state layer.
- Tag every topic and every news item to the syllabus.
