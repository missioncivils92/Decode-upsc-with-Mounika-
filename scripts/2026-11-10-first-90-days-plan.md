## Hook
[On screen: "Day 1. 10 tabs open. 5 Telegram groups. 0 pages read."]

Day one of UPSC preparation usually looks like this. Ten tabs open. Five Telegram groups joined. Three booklists downloaded. And not one page actually read.

I've mentored UPSC and TGPSC aspirants for five years, and the first 90 days decide more than people think. Get them right and you have a habit, a base, and confidence. Get them wrong and you're still "planning" in March.

So here is a simple 90-day plan. Three phases, thirty days each. No overload.

## Script

### 1. Before day one: three rules (0:50)
[On screen: three rules]

Rule one: one source per subject. Not three. You can add later; you can't un-confuse yourself.

Rule two: consistency beats hours. Four honest hours every day beats twelve hours on Sunday and nothing on Monday.

Rule three: the syllabus is your boss. If you haven't seen my video putting the syllabus on one page, watch it first, and download the PDF. Everything in this plan hangs on it.

How many hours? If you're full-time, aim for six focused hours. If you're working, two to three on weekdays and more on weekends is enough to start. Focused means phone in another room.

### 2. Phase 1, Days 1–30: Foundation (2:10)
[On screen: "PHASE 1 · FOUNDATION"]

Goal: understand the exam, build the reading habit, and lay the NCERT base.

Week 1: the exam itself. Read the official notification. Print the syllabus. Look at last year's Prelims question paper; not to solve it, just to see what UPSC asks. It will scare you a little. That's fine; it gives you direction.

Weeks 2 to 4: NCERTs. These are school textbooks from NCERT, and they're the base of everything. Start with three subjects:
[On screen: list]
- History: the Class 6 to 8 Social Science history books, then Class 12 "Themes in Indian History".
- Geography: Class 11 "Fundamentals of Physical Geography".
- Polity: Class 11 "Indian Constitution at Work".

Read them like a story. Don't make heavy notes yet. Just underline.

Every day, from day one: the newspaper. 45 minutes, not more. The Hindu or The Indian Express. Read only what connects to the syllabus: government, economy, environment, international relations, science. Skip politics-as-gossip, crime and sports.

[On screen: "Day 30 check ✓"]
By day 30 you should have: read the syllabus, seen one PYQ paper, finished at least three NCERTs, and read the paper for 30 days straight.

### 3. Phase 2, Days 31–60: Go deep in one subject (5:00)
[On screen: "PHASE 2 · ONE SUBJECT DEEP"]

Goal: master one core subject end to end. I recommend Polity first. Why? It's in Prelims, in GS II, and in every State PSC exam. It's also the most structured subject; it builds your confidence.

The standard book is "Indian Polity" by M. Laxmikanth. Here's how to read it:
[On screen: three steps]
One, read a chapter. Two, make short notes, one page per chapter, in your own words. Three, solve the Prelims PYQs from that chapter the same day.

That third step is the one most beginners skip. PYQs, previous year questions, tell you which parts of a chapter UPSC actually cares about. Use them from day one, not in the last month.

Continue the newspaper. Now start a simple current-affairs notebook: one line per story, tagged to the syllabus. "Supreme Court on governor's powers: GS II, federalism." That's it.

Saturday is revision day: reread the week's notes. Sunday, watch my weekly current affairs roundup and fill gaps in your notebook.

[On screen: "Day 60 check ✓"]
By day 60: Polity finished once, one-page notes for every chapter, chapter-wise PYQs done.

### 4. Phase 3, Days 61–90: Add a subject, start writing (7:30)
[On screen: "PHASE 3 · ADD & WRITE"]

Goal: start the second subject and begin answer writing.

Second subject: Economy or Modern History. Pick one. For Economy, the Class 11 and 12 NCERT Economics books first. For Modern History, the Class 12 NCERT and then one standard modern history book.

Now, answer writing. I've written Mains twice, and if there's one thing I wish I'd started earlier, it's this. Start now. Not "after I finish the syllabus".
[On screen: "1 answer a day · 150 words · 7 minutes"]
One Mains question a day. From Polity, since you've finished it. 150 words. Seven minutes. Write by hand. Introduction, body in points, conclusion. It will be bad. Every first answer is. Write it anyway.

And take your first test: one sectional Prelims test on Polity. Not a full mock; just Polity. Check your score and, more importantly, why you got each wrong answer wrong.

[On screen: "Day 90 check ✓"]
By day 90: Polity revised twice, a second subject half done, 30 answers written, one sectional test analysed, and 90 days of newspaper reading. That is a real foundation.

### 5. If you're also preparing for Group 1 (9:30)
[On screen: "+ APPSC / TGPSC"]

If you're preparing for APPSC or TGPSC Group 1 too, follow the same plan. Polity, Economy, History: the same core serves both. Just add one evening a week for your state: state history, state schemes, the state budget. When a state notification is released, we'll shift gears together; I'll cover that in Thursday's video.

### 6. Five mistakes that kill the first 90 days (10:15)
[On screen: list, one at a time]
1. Collecting resources instead of reading them.
2. Choosing your optional in week one. Wait at least three months.
3. Watching videos without writing anything down. Including mine.
4. Comparing your day 20 with someone's year 2.
5. Skipping revision. If you read it once, you'll forget most of it within a month.

### 7. Recap (11:45)
[On screen: 3-phase timeline]
- Days 1–30: exam, syllabus, NCERTs, newspaper habit.
- Days 31–60: Polity, end to end, with notes and PYQs.
- Days 61–90: second subject, one answer a day, first sectional test.

### 8. Next step (12:30)
Your task for today: write your start date on top of the syllabus page, and write "Day 90" with the date three months from now. That's your first milestone.
