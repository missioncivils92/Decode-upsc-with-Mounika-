## 2. UPSC Civil Services Examination (CSE)

### 2.1 What it recruits for
About 20+ All India and Central services through one exam, including IAS, IPS, IFS (Foreign Service), IRS and other Group A/B central services. The Indian Forest Service (IFoS) uses CSE Prelims as its screening test.

### 2.2 Three stages

**Stage 1: Preliminary (objective, screening only)**
| Paper | Questions | Marks | Time | Counts for merit? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GS Paper I | 100 | 200 | 2 hr | Decides the cut-off |
| GS Paper II (CSAT) | 80 | 200 | 2 hr | Qualifying only (33%) |

- Negative marking: one-third of the question's marks per wrong answer.
- Prelims marks do not count in the final ranking.

**Stage 2: Mains (descriptive, 9 papers)**
| Paper | Marks | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Paper A: Indian language | 300 | Qualifying (Telugu is an option) |
| Paper B: English | 300 | Qualifying |
| Essay | 250 | Counted |
| GS I: Heritage, culture, history, geography, society | 250 | Counted |
| GS II: Polity, governance, social justice, IR | 250 | Counted |
| GS III: Economy, environment, S&T, security, disaster mgmt | 250 | Counted |
| GS IV: Ethics, integrity, aptitude | 250 | Counted |
| Optional I & II | 250 + 250 | Counted (one optional subject, about 48 to choose from, including Telugu literature) |

Counted written total: **1,750**.

**Stage 3: Personality Test (interview):** 275 marks. Final merit is out of **2,025**.

### 2.3 Eligibility (standard rules; confirm against the current notification)
- Any graduate degree. Final-year students may sit Prelims.
- Age 21 to 32 for General, with relaxation: OBC +3, SC/ST +5, PwBD further relaxation.
- Attempts: General/EWS 6, OBC 9, SC/ST unlimited up to the age limit. Appearing in even one Prelims paper uses an attempt.
- **New in CSE 2026:** candidates already serving in IAS or IFS cannot appear. IPS officers may appear but cannot choose IPS again. Candidates allotted IPS or a Group A service through CSE 2026 get one improvement attempt in CSE 2027 by taking a one-time training exemption.

### 2.4 Current cycle status (as of 26 Oct 2026)
| Cycle | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **CSE 2025** | ~9.38 lakh applied, ~5.77 lakh appeared in Prelims, 14,161 qualified for Mains, **958 recommended** (final result 6 Mar 2026). That is ~2.5% of Prelims takers clearing Prelims and ~0.1% of applicants selected. |
| **CSE 2026** | Notification 4 Feb 2026, ~933 vacancies (33 PwBD). Prelims 24 May 2026; ~5.49 lakh appeared, **13,343 qualified** (result ~15 Jun 2026). Mains from 21 Aug 2026. Mains result awaited. Aspirants described the 2026 Prelims as hard and unfamiliar. |
| **CSE 2027** | Notification **13 Jan 2027**, apply by **2 Feb 2027**, Prelims **23 May 2027**, Mains from **20 Aug 2027** (UPSC calendar released 20 May 2026; dates "liable to alteration"). |

**Implication for beginners:** a person starting today has about 7 months to Prelims 2027. Realistic first serious attempt for most true beginners is **CSE 2028**, with 2027 as an optional "experience" attempt only if they accept it uses an attempt.

### 2.5 The annual rhythm (use for the content calendar)
| Month | What aspirants are doing |
|---|---|
| Jan–Feb | Notification, form filling (high search interest: "how to fill UPSC form") |
| Mar–May | Prelims revision, mocks, CSAT panic |
| Late May | Prelims day; answer key and "expected cut-off" searches spike |
| Jun | Prelims result; new beginners start for next year |
| Jul–Sep | Mains (Aug); answer writing, essay |
| Oct–Dec | Mains result; interview prep; beginners planning next year |
| Jan–Apr | Interviews; final result (Mar–Apr); topper-strategy season |

## 3. State PSCs: Andhra Pradesh and Telangana

Patterns below are from aggregator sites and the last notified cycles. **Sources disagree on details. Always confirm against the current official notification before stating them in a video.**

### 3.1 APPSC Group 1 (AP)
- Prelims: 2 objective papers × 120 marks, 2 hr each. Screening only. Negative marking reported as 1/3 by most sources, but at least one says none. Verify.
- Mains: descriptive papers (qualifying Telugu and English plus counted GS-type papers including Essay, History/Culture, Polity, Economy, S&T). Exact count and marks need confirming.
- Interview: status varies by cycle; one source says AP has dropped interviews for upcoming recruitments. Verify.
- Next notification: aggregators expected ~Aug 2026 (~91 posts); not confirmed officially.

### 3.2 APPSC Group 2 (AP)
- Prelims: 1 objective paper, 150 Q / 150 marks / 150 min, GS & Mental Ability, 1/3 negative marking.
- Mains: 2 objective papers × 150 marks (Paper 1: social history of AP + Indian Constitution; Paper 2: Indian & AP economy + S&T).
- Computer Proficiency Test: qualifying.

### 3.3 TGPSC Group 1 (Telangana), last cycle (Notification 02/2024, 563 posts)
- Prelims: 1 objective paper, 150 questions, GS & Mental Ability (screening).
- Mains: General English (qualifying, Class X standard) + **6 papers × 150 = 900 marks**, 3 hr each:
  1. General Essay
  2. History, Culture & Geography
  3. Indian Society, Constitution & Governance
  4. Economy & Development
  5. Science & Technology and Data Interpretation
  6. Telangana Movement & State Formation
- Medium: English, Telugu or Urdu.
- New notification: as of May 2026 TGPSC was collecting vacancies; no notification confirmed yet.

### 3.4 Overlap that makes one channel work for both
UPSC GS and State PSC GS share roughly 60–70% of the core: Polity, Economy, Modern History, Geography, Environment, S&T, current affairs. State papers add **state history, state movement (Telangana statehood), state economy, state schemes, AP Reorganisation Act 2014 issues.** So most explainers can serve both, with a short "State PSC angle" segment.

## 4. The audience: beginner aspirants

### 4.1 Who they are
- Final-year students and fresh graduates (roughly 20–24), plus some young working professionals.
- Many from Tier-2/3 towns; many first-generation aspirants with no one at home who knows the exam.
- For this channel, a meaningful share from AP and Telangana who also keep Group 1/2 as a parallel or backup path.
- Watch on phones, often on mobile data; heavy Telegram users for PDFs and notes.

### 4.2 What they struggle with
1. **"Where do I start?"** Syllabus feels huge; no sequence.
2. **Information overload.** Too many channels, booklists and strategies; they can't tell what to trust.
3. **Passive watching.** Videos feel productive but nothing sticks; no notes, no revision.
4. **No answer-writing practice.** Video-only prep leaves them unable to write Mains answers.
5. **Current affairs anxiety.** Fear of missing news; don't know how to link news to syllabus.
6. **Time management and burnout**, especially for working professionals and repeat watchers who never start.
7. **UPSC vs State PSC confusion.** Whether to prepare for both, and how.
8. **Exam unpredictability.** Hard, unfamiliar Prelims (e.g., 2026) shakes confidence.
9. **Money.** Coaching costs lakhs; they want free, credible guidance.

### 4.3 What they search for (evergreen beginner queries)
"UPSC syllabus explained", "how to start UPSC preparation from zero", "UPSC booklist for beginners", "NCERTs for UPSC", "how to read newspaper for UPSC", "UPSC without coaching", "UPSC for working professionals", "UPSC vs Group 1", "how to choose optional", "CSAT preparation", "UPSC eligibility age attempts", "UPSC exam pattern", "how to make notes for UPSC", "PYQ analysis".

### 4.4 Competitive landscape
- Big institutional channels (Drishti IAS ~12.8M subs, Vision IAS ~1.8M, UPSC Wallah ~1.6M) dominate daily current affairs and long lectures, mostly Hindi-led or institution-branded.
- **Gap for this channel:** a single, credible, English-language mentor voice that is **beginner-first**, **calm and structured** (anti-overload), and **bridges UPSC with APPSC/TGPSC**. Big channels rarely serve that South-Indian dual-track beginner well.

---

## 5. Key takeaways for the channel
1. Lead with clarity, not volume. "Decode" means turning chaos into a sequence.
2. Every explainer links static syllabus + current affair + PYQ + a practice question.
3. Add a 60–90 second "APPSC/TGPSC angle" where relevant.
4. Be honest about odds (~0.1% selection) and push a backup plan; this builds trust.
5. Verify every date, number and rule against official sites. Coaching sites contradict each other.
